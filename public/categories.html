GEO categories

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) measures how well your content is positioned to be understood, trusted, and cited by AI-powered search tools and chat assistants. Unlike traditional SEO, which focuses on rankings, GEO focuses on the qualities that make content useful in AI-generated answers. The categories below evaluate seven key factors that influence AI visibility: relevance, authority, clarity, conversational fit, uniqueness, engagement, and trustworthiness. Together, they provide a practical view of how effectively your content answers questions, demonstrates expertise, delivers value, and builds credibility. Use these explanations to understand what each category measures, how your score is determined, and what improvements can increase the likelihood of your content being referenced and recommended by AI systems.

1. Relevance

Relevance measures how well your content answers the questions visitors are likely searching for. It reflects whether your page stays focused on its topic and provides the information people expect to find. Content that directly addresses user needs is more likely to be used by AI assistants when generating answers.

Evaluation Criteria

This category evaluates whether the content matches search intent, answers common questions, stays on topic, and covers the subject in sufficient depth. Pages that provide clear, complete answers score higher than pages that contain mostly promotional or unrelated information.

Low vs High Score Examples

Low score: A page about running shoes that mainly discusses company history and marketing messages.
High score: A page that explains how to choose running shoes, compares different options, answers common questions, and provides practical buying advice.

2. Authority

Authority measures whether your content demonstrates genuine expertise on a topic. AI systems are more likely to trust and reference content that appears knowledgeable, confident, and backed by experience. Strong authority helps establish your website as a reliable source of information.

Evaluation Criteria

This category looks for expert-level knowledge, confident explanations, clear reasoning, and evidence that the author understands the subject. Vague statements and superficial explanations typically result in lower scores.

Low vs High Score Examples

Low score: "Our product is the best and works great for everyone."
High score: "This shoe uses additional cushioning to reduce impact during long-distance runs, making it suitable for beginner and recreational runners."

3. Clarity

Clarity measures how easy your content is to read and understand. Even valuable information can be overlooked if it is confusing, poorly structured, or difficult to follow. AI systems also prefer content that presents information in a clear and organized way.

Evaluation Criteria

This category evaluates sentence structure, readability, logical organization, formatting, and the overall flow of information. Content with clear sections, headings, and concise explanations receives higher scores.

Low vs High Score Examples

Low score: Large walls of text with no headings, repetitive language, and unclear explanations.
High score: Well-organized content with descriptive headings, short paragraphs, and straightforward explanations.

4. Conversational Fit

Conversational Fit measures how easily your content can be used in AI-generated conversations and question-and-answer responses. Since AI assistants often provide direct answers, content structured around answering questions tends to perform better.

Evaluation Criteria

This category looks for direct answers, FAQ sections, question-based headings, concise explanations, and content that can easily be extracted into conversational responses.

Low vs High Score Examples

Low score: A product page containing only marketing copy and sales messages.
High score: Content that includes questions such as "How do I choose the right running shoe?" followed by a clear and direct answer.

5. Uniqueness

Uniqueness measures how original and distinctive your content is compared to generic information available elsewhere online. AI systems are more likely to cite content that offers fresh insights, specific expertise, or detailed explanations rather than generic marketing statements.

Evaluation Criteria

This category evaluates originality, avoidance of repetitive content, unique insights, and whether the page provides information beyond standard boilerplate text.

Low vs High Score Examples

Low score: "We provide high-quality products and excellent customer service."
High score: Detailed explanations, proprietary research, expert recommendations, case studies, or first-hand experience that cannot easily be found elsewhere.

6. Engagement

Engagement measures how effectively your content keeps visitors interested and encourages them to continue reading. Content that is useful, actionable, and easy to consume is more likely to be referenced by AI systems and appreciated by users.

Evaluation Criteria

This category evaluates the use of examples, actionable advice, practical explanations, lists, educational content, and overall usefulness to the reader.

Low vs High Score Examples

Low score: Short, generic content with little detail and no practical guidance.
High score: Content that includes examples, step-by-step instructions, comparisons, FAQs, and practical recommendations that help users solve a problem.

7. Trustworthiness

Trustworthiness measures whether visitors and AI systems can rely on the information presented on your site. Content that provides evidence, references, author information, and verifiable claims is more likely to be considered credible and cited in AI-generated responses.

Evaluation Criteria

This category evaluates supporting evidence, citations, references, factual accuracy, author transparency, and the absence of exaggerated or unsupported claims.

Low vs High Score Examples

Low score: "Experts agree this is the best solution available" without supporting evidence.
High score: Content that references research, includes expert authors, cites sources, shows publish dates, and supports claims with verifiable facts.

13 SEO Audit Rules

The 13 core SEO checks below are used in our analysis to evaulate the SEO score of a website. These checks cover essential on-page, content, technical, and trust signals, including page titles, meta descriptions, headings, image accessibility, content quality, internal linking, URL structure, and structured data. Together, they provide a clear picture of how well your pages are optimized for search visibility and user experience. Improving these areas can help search engines better understand your content, increase organic traffic, improve engagement, and make it easier for visitors to find the information they need. Use the explanations below to understand each rule, why it matters, and how to strengthen your website's overall SEO performance.

1. Title Tag

The title tag is the clickable headline that appears in search engine results and browser tabs. It tells both search engines and visitors what a page is about. A strong title clearly describes the page and includes the main topic people are searching for.

What it measures

This rule checks whether your page has a title tag and whether its length is between 30 and 60 characters. Titles that are too short may lack important context, while titles that are too long can be cut off in search results.

Why it matters

The title tag is often the first thing potential visitors see before deciding whether to click. A clear, descriptive title can improve click-through rates and help search engines understand the page topic. Missing, vague, or overly long titles can reduce visibility in search results and make it harder for users to choose your page over competitors.

Example

Running Shoes for Men & Women | ASICS Netherlands

2. Meta Description

A meta description is the short summary that often appears beneath your page title in search results. It gives people a preview of what they will find on the page and encourages them to click.

What it measures

This rule checks whether a meta description exists and whether it is between 120 and 160 characters. Descriptions that are too short may not provide enough information, while longer descriptions may be truncated.

Why it matters

Although meta descriptions are not a direct ranking factor, they strongly influence whether people click on your listing. A compelling description can increase traffic by making your page stand out from competitors. Missing or poorly written descriptions can lead to lower click rates and lost opportunities, even if your page ranks well.

Example

Shop ASICS running shoes and sportswear in the Netherlands. Free delivery on orders over €35. Explore the complete collection today.

3. Single H1 Tag

The H1 is the main headline of your page. It introduces the primary topic and helps visitors quickly understand what the page is about.

What it measures

This rule checks whether a page contains exactly one H1 heading. Having no H1 or multiple H1 headings can create confusion about the page's main focus.

Why it matters

A single, clear H1 improves page structure for both visitors and search engines. It helps search engines understand the content and gives users confidence that they have landed on the right page. Missing or multiple H1 headings can dilute the page's focus and make the content harder to navigate.

Example

Official ASICS Running Shoes

4. H2 Heading Structure

H2 headings divide content into clear sections. They make long pages easier to read by breaking information into manageable topics.

What it measures

This rule checks whether the page contains at least two H2 headings that organize the content into meaningful sections.

Why it matters

Well-structured content improves readability and helps visitors find the information they need faster. Search engines also use headings to understand page topics and subtopics. Pages with few or no subheadings can appear disorganized and may struggle to rank for a wider range of related searches.

Example

How to Choose the Right Running Shoe, Road vs Trail Running Shoes, and Finding Your Correct Shoe Size.

5. Image Alt Text

Alt text is a short description attached to an image. It explains the image when it cannot be seen and helps search engines understand visual content.

What it measures

This rule checks whether every image on the page includes descriptive alt text. Missing alt attributes will cause the rule to fail.

Why it matters

Alt text improves accessibility for visitors using screen readers and provides context when images fail to load. It can also help your images appear in image search results. Pages with missing alt text lose an opportunity to improve accessibility, visibility, and overall content quality.

Example

alt="Woman wearing ASICS Gel-Nimbus running shoes during a road run"

6. Content Depth (Minimum 300 Words)

Content depth measures whether a page contains enough information to answer visitors' questions and explain its topic properly.

What it measures

This rule checks whether the page contains at least 300 words of meaningful content.

Why it matters

Pages with very little content often struggle to rank because they provide limited value to visitors. More comprehensive content allows you to explain benefits, answer common questions, and address search intent more effectively. Thin content can result in lower rankings, fewer visitors, and reduced trust from both users and search engines.

Example

A product page includes detailed features, benefits, sizing guidance, care instructions, and frequently asked questions.

7. Canonical Tag

A canonical tag tells search engines which version of a page should be treated as the primary version when similar or duplicate pages exist.

What it measures

This rule checks whether the page contains a canonical URL that points to the preferred version of the page.

Why it matters

Duplicate content can occur when the same page is accessible through multiple URLs. Without a canonical tag, search engines may split ranking signals across these versions, reducing visibility. A canonical tag consolidates authority into one preferred page and helps search engines index the correct URL.

Example

All duplicate product URLs point to https://www.example.com/running-shoes/ as the preferred version.

8. Schema Markup (JSON-LD)

Schema markup is structured information that helps search engines understand your content more clearly. It provides additional details about your business, products, articles, and other page types.

What it measures

This rule checks whether structured data (JSON-LD schema markup) is present on the page.

Why it matters

Schema markup can improve how your pages appear in search results and helps search engines interpret your content more accurately. It also increases the likelihood that AI-powered search experiences can understand, reference, and present your content. Pages without schema may miss visibility opportunities available to competitors.

Example

A product page uses Product schema to provide details such as the product name, price, availability, and customer reviews.

9. Internal Links (Minimum 2)

Internal links connect one page on your website to another. They help visitors discover related content and guide them through your site.

What it measures

This rule checks whether the page contains at least two links pointing to other pages within the same website.

Why it matters

Internal links make it easier for search engines to discover and understand your content. They also encourage visitors to explore more pages, increasing engagement and potential conversions. Pages with few or no internal links can become isolated, making them harder for both users and search engines to find.

Example

A running shoe page links to Men's Running Shoes, Women's Running Shoes, and a Running Shoe Size Guide.

10. Clean URL Structure

A clean URL is short, descriptive, and easy to understand. It gives users and search engines a clear indication of what the page contains.

What it measures

This rule checks whether the URL is reasonably short and does not contain unnecessary query parameters.

Why it matters

Clear URLs are easier to read, share, and remember. They improve user trust and make search results look more professional. Long, confusing URLs can reduce clarity and make pages appear less relevant. A cleanURL also helps search engines better understand the page topic.

Example

Use example.com/mens-running-shoes instead of example.com/page?id=123&cat=shoes.

11. Author Attribution

Author attribution identifies who created or reviewed the content. It shows visitors that real people stand behind the information.

What it measures

This rule checks whether an author name is provided for the page, particularly for articles, guides, and blog content.

Why it matters

People are more likely to trust content when they know who wrote it. Author information can demonstrate expertise, authority, and credibility, especially for topics where accuracy matters. Pages without visible authorship may appear less trustworthy to visitors and may be less likely to be referenced by AI-powered search experiences.

Example

Written by Dr. Sarah van den Berg, Sports Physiotherapist, with a linked author profile and credentials.

12. Publish / Update Date

Publish and update dates show when content was created and when it was last reviewed or revised.

What it measures

This rule checks whether the page includes publication and/or last-updated dates in its content or metadata.

Why it matters

Users prefer current information, especially when making purchasing decisions or researching a topic. Fresh content signals that a website is maintained and trustworthy. Pages without dates may appear outdated or unreliable, causing visitors to leave and reducing confidence in the information provided.

Example

Published: 15 January 2025.
Last updated: 10 May 2025.

13. FAQ Schema (FAQPage)

FAQ schema adds structured question-and-answer content to a page, making it easier for search engines and AI systems to understand common customer questions.

What it measures

This rule checks whether FAQPage schema markup has been added to pages that contain frequently asked questions.

Why it matters

FAQs directly address customer concerns and can increase trust, engagement, and conversions. Structured FAQ markup helps search engines understand these questions and answers more clearly. It can also improve the chances that AI-powered search tools will reference your content when answering user questions. Without FAQ schema, valuable information may be harder to surface and reuse.

Example

A running shoe page could include:

Q: What is the best running shoe for beginners?
A: The ASICS Gel-Nimbus is a popular choice because it offers excellent cushioning and comfort.

Adding FAQ schema ensures search engines can clearly identify this question-and-answer content.
